Chapter 9 · Comparable samples of a network

Sampling a network: what your sample hides

Learning objective: Compare node, edge and snowball samples of a network and see how each biases degree and density.

LC 01 · observe

At a 20% random-node sample (seed 1), what is the sample mean degree vs the true mean degree?

Read "sample mean degree" and "true mean degree" in the Metrics panel at baseline.
0.52 vs 3.97, a bias of about −87%. An edge survives a node sample only if BOTH its counties are drawn, so roughly 0.2 × 0.2 = 4% of flows survive while 20% of counties do. Degree is cut far more than proportionally.

LC 02 · intervene

Switch to snowball sampling from the marked county. Which way does mean degree move and why?

Watch the delta on "sample mean degree" and where the lit counties sit on the map.
Mean degree rises from 0.52 to 4.19 (+6% against the truth), and density jumps to about +435%: the snowball keeps 85 counties that are all near each other, so nearly every flow among them survives. It fixes degree but overstates how tightly knit the network is.

LC 03 · transfer

You must compare evacuation flows between two storms, each with a sample. Which sampling method would you use, and why?

Change the seed under each method. Which bias stays stable, and which depends on where you start?
Comparable samples need the same design. A random node or edge sample at a fixed fraction biases every storm the same way, so differences between storms are real. A snowball's bias depends on the start county, which differs between storms.
