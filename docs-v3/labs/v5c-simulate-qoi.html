LC 01 · observe

At the default scenario (50,000 people, income 1200, all towns, 1000 simulations), what is the 95% simulation interval for expected emissions?

Read "sim 2.5%" and "sim 97.5%" in the sidebar: they are the percentiles of the histogram.
413.3 to 434.1: the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles of 1000 simulated expected values. The analytic interval from predict() is close, 413.1 to 435.2.

LC 02 · intervene

Refit on a 20% subsample. How do the cloud of betas and the interval change?

Watch "sim width" and the axes of the beta cloud when you pick 20%.
With 347 towns instead of 1737 the vcov is larger, so the betas scatter more and the simulation interval widens from about 20.8 to about 48.5. More simulations only smooth the histogram; they do not narrow it.

LC 03 · transfer

Why simulate a quantity of interest instead of reporting the coefficient table alone?

Switch to First difference: is there a predict() interval for it?
A log-log coefficient is hard to read; "doubling a 50,000-person town adds about 383 kt CO2 (367 to 399)" is not. Simulation carries the joint uncertainty of all betas into any QoI, including a first difference where predict() gives no interval.
