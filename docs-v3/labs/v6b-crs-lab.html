Why distances lie: the same polling places in three CRSs

LC 01 · observe

What distance does EPSG:4326 report between the two marked polling places, and what does EPSG:26986 report?

Switch the CRS to EPSG:4326 and read "distance (m)" and "% error". Then try EPSG:3857.
EPSG:4326 reports 5,344.52 m (sf hands lon/lat to s2, which measures on a sphere); EPSG:26986 reports 5,341.27 m, a 0.06% gap. The CRS that really lies is EPSG:3857: 7,238.96 m, 35.5% too long, because web Mercator stretches everything by 1/cos(latitude), about 1.35 in Boston.

LC 02 · intervene

Buffer by 0.01 in EPSG:4326 (choose "0.01 in degrees"), then view it in EPSG:26986. Why is the buffer an ellipse on the projected map?

Compare with "1 km in metres": that buffer stays round in the state plane. What is 0.01 of a degree, east-west versus north-south?
In lon/lat, 0.01° is a circle only in degree space. On the ground a degree of longitude shrinks with cos(latitude): 0.01° is about 822 m east-west but 1,111 m north-south in Boston, so the zone is a tall ellipse and its "radius" is not a distance at all.

LC 03 · transfer

A classmate computed service areas around polling places in EPSG:4326. What would you check, and what would you change?

Metres are necessary but not enough: look at the % error EPSG:3857 shows here.
Assert the CRS first (st_crs()), then st_transform(crs = 26986) and buffer with dist = 1000. EPSG:3857 has metre units but overstates this distance by 35.5% (and areas by about 84%), so it is not a fix.
