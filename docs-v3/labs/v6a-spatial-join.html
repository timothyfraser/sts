LC 01 · observe

At baseline, how many block groups contain zero social-infrastructure sites?

Read "block groups with no site" in the sidebar, or count the dashed outlines. The aggregate table cannot show them yet: a block group with no site never appears on the sites side of the join.
384 of the 680 block groups hold no site. The 51 is the other direction of emptiness: sites that fall in no block group. And the count-of-0 rows are 0 at baseline only because the join starts from the sites.

LC 02 · intervene

Switch the direction to block groups → sites with a left join. Why does the row count change?

Watch "rows after join" and "unmatched rows". Which table does a left join never drop rows from?
Rows go from 1,051 to 1,384. Both directions share the same 1,000 matched pairs (intersects is symmetric); what changes is which side's unmatched rows the left join keeps: 51 sites before, 384 block groups after. Only this direction can count the zeros.

LC 03 · transfer

A city has 2 million points in a PostGIS table. When should the spatial join run in PostGIS instead of in R after collecting?

Compare the size of what each option sends over the connection: 2 million point rows, or one row per block group.
Push the join and the GROUP BY to the data. Here the aggregate is at most 680 rows for 1,049 sites; at 2 million points it is still one row per polygon, and a GiST index on geom keeps the match fast. Collect in R when you need the geometry-level rows or the data already fits in memory.
