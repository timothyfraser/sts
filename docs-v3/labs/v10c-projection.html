LC 01 · observe

At baseline (people, count shared, threshold 1), how many edges does the people projection have?

Read "edges" in the sidebar. The two-mode network has 90 membership ties; the projection counts pairs of people.
802. Each committee of n people becomes a clique of n(n − 1)/2 edges: 90 seats turn into 802 person-person edges, density 0.230.

LC 02 · intervene

Remove the largest committee (committee_35, 25 members). What happens to density, and why so much?

A committee of 25 makes 25 × 24 / 2 = 300 pairs. Watch the delta next to "edges".
Edges drop from 802 to 505 and density from 0.230 to 0.145, a 37% fall from removing 28% of the seats. Edges grow with the square of committee size, so the biggest committee dominates the projection.

LC 03 · transfer

Why can a projected network overstate how connected people are?

Raise the threshold to 2 and see how many edges survive.
A shared committee is assumed to be a tie. Only 3 of the 802 edges rest on two or more shared committees; the rest are one co-membership each. Thresholds and Newman weights (total weight 45 instead of 805) discount big committees.
