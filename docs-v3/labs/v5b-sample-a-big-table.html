Sampling from a big table: the sampling distribution

LC 01 · observe

With n = 25 and 1,000 samples, what is the standard error of the sample mean, observed vs sd/sqrt(n)?

Read "SE observed" and "SE = sd/sqrt(n)" in the sidebar. The SE is the spread of the blue histogram, not of the grey one.
The 1,000 sample means have sd 8.02 minutes; the formula gives 40.21 / sqrt(25) = 8.04. The population sd (40.2) is the spread of single flights; dividing by n instead of sqrt(n) shrinks it far too much.

LC 02 · intervene

Quadruple n from 25 to 100. What happens to the SE, and to the shape of the sample-means histogram?

sqrt(4) = 2. Turn on the normal curve and compare the fit at n = 25 and n = 100.
SE observed drops from 8.02 to 4.11 (formula: 8.04 to 4.02), about half, because SE scales with 1/sqrt(n). The means pile up tighter and their skew fades, so the normal curve fits better.

LC 03 · transfer

State the central limit theorem correctly. What converges to what, and what does the law of large numbers say instead?

The grey population histogram never changes shape. What does change as n grows: the shape of the blue histogram, or where it is centred?
The departures stay right-skewed at every n (up to a 1,301-minute delay). What turns normal is the distribution of the 1,000 means (CLT), with spread 40.2/sqrt(n). Separately, each mean lands ever closer to the population mean of 12.64 minutes (LLN): the blue histogram's centre is about 13.05 at n = 25 and 12.98 at n = 100.
