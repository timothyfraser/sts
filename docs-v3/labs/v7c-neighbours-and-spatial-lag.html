LC 01 · observe

Under queen contiguity, how many neighbours does the selected municipality (Maebashi) have?

Count the yellow links on the map, or read "neighbours of selected" in the sidebar. "Mean links" is the prefecture average, not Maebashi's count.
8. Maebashi shares a border point with 8 municipalities, so its spatial lag adj_sp is the mean of those 8 values: 1,323.9 solar farms, against its own 3,174.

LC 02 · intervene

Switch the rule to k = 3 nearest. What happens to Moran's I, and why?

Moran's I is a property of the data AND the weights W. Watch the slope and the delta next to Moran's I.
Moran's I falls from 0.253 to 0.222. With k = 3 every lag is a mean of the 3 closest centroids (Maebashi's lag drops to 750), so the lag column changes and so does its slope. It still sits just above the permutation band (upper edge 0.216), so the clustering is still unlikely to be chance. Try k = 6: I falls to 0.124.

LC 03 · transfer

In chapter 12 you predict with spatial features. Why does spatially correlated error matter there?

If neighbours share a value, they share an error too. What does a random split put on both sides of the line?
A positive Moran's I on the residuals (like the 0.253 here on raw counts) means each error predicts its neighbours' errors. The model effectively has fewer independent observations than rows, and a random split leaks neighbours into the test set, so fit looks better than it is. Check residuals with moran.test() and split by space.
