LC 01 · observe

At cell size 5 km, how many cells contain no site at all?

Set the method to "none" so only cells that hold a site are coloured, then read "empty cells" in Metrics.
40 of the 56 cells hold no site; only 16 cells carry a measurement. Interpolation colours the other 40, but it does not measure them.

LC 02 · intervene

Switch from nearest neighbour to IDW power 2, then power 6. What happens to leave-one-out RMSE and to the map's texture?

Watch "LOO RMSE" as you change the method and power at hour 0. A very high power makes each cell copy its closest site.
At hour 0, nearest neighbour gives RMSE 4.39 µg/m³, IDW power 2 gives 3.08 and power 6 gives 3.98. Power 2 blends neighbours into a smooth map; power 6 behaves almost like nearest neighbour, with blocky patches around each site.

LC 03 · transfer

Why does a smaller grid cell not always give a "more accurate" map? Relate your answer to the modifiable areal unit problem.

Compare 2 km and 5 km: read "empty cells", "mean of site cells" and "LOO RMSE".
At 2 km, 297 of 320 cells are empty, against 40 of 56 at 5 km, and the mean of the site cells moves from 6.58 to 7.18 µg/m³ just because the zones changed. Leave-one-out RMSE stays at 3.08: the error comes from the points and the method, not the cell size. That dependence of the summary on the zoning is the modifiable areal unit problem.
