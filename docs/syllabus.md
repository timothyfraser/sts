### COURSE DESCRIPTION

Modern systems frequently involve and rely on databases to record,
share, and route big data, such as electrical grids, airlines, public
transit, bike sharing, banking, security, and software systems, among
many others. But to build, query, and communicate data from these
databases, systems engineers need foundational skills in relational
databases, systems for interfacing with this data (dashboards), and an
open source programming language for working with these systems.

This course trains participants to query and visualize big-data at
scale, with a topical focus on socio-technical systems. Course contents
include training in building and using relational databases, data
visualization, and data communication with dashboard, focusing on
several common big data formats, including *panel/time-series data*,
*geographic information systems*, and *network data*. Participants will
learn and apply these applied to several important kinds of
socio-technical systems, involving **transportation and urban mobility, renewable energy, disaster resilience, and social infrastructure**. They will learn to analyze these systems
using database techniques, quantify these systems' performance using
metrics suitable for socio-technical systems, and communicate their
findings for non-experts, with an emphasis on data-driven
decision-making for public facing technologies. In particular,
participants will learn to communicate their findings using dashboard
systems, including best practices for data visualization and making
reactive, production-grade data products. No prior coding or statistical
experience is required; by the end of the course, participants will
demonstrate mastery of core techniques for databases, visualization, and
dashboards.

### *COURSE LEARNING OUTCOMES*

- **Analyze Big Datasets and Databases.** Participants will use data
  wrangling techniques and SQL/SQLite to process social-technical
  systems data at scale, using spatial and network techniques.

- **Make Data-Driven Decisions**. This includes the measurement and
  design of key metrics to enable managers to make data-driven decisions
  that meet stakeholder needs.

- **Communicate and Visualize Data Effectively.** Participants will
  learn methods for advanced data communication techniques and their
  tradeoffs when communicating findings to the public.

- **Build Dashboard Interfaces for Databases.** Participants will learn
  how to systematically architect and build dashboard systems to
  interface with databases, from mapping their process to implementation
  and testing.

- **Evaluate Socio-Technical Data**. Skills include drawing inferences
  from big data over time, spatial data, and network data.

## Should I take this course?

### WHO IS THIS COURSE FOR?

Do any of the following apply to you? These types of participants may
find this course especially helpful for reaching your professional
goals.

- Systems engineers who want to improve their **data science, data visualization, and data communication skills**!

- Engineers who want to become proficient in **using relational databases** and data wrangling.

- Engineers interested in **software systems** - you'll learn
  to architect and build your own **dashboard interface** -
  managing inputs, outputs, processes, as a great example of engineering
  your own system.

- Analysts who want to make reproducible, **publication-quality visuals**, maps, and more.

- Engineers and analysts who want to become proficient in an
  **open-source programming language**.

- Engineers who need to work with complex data formats, including **temporal data, spatial data, and network data**.

- Engineers and analysts who work on **socio-technical systems**, including (but not limited to) transportation,
  mobility, energy, public health, banking, security, or
  workforce-relevant systems. (Although skills learned will be
  transferable to other system types too.)

### WHY THIS COURSE?

Systems engineers today often must work with databases and statistical
techniques, but may struggle to convey their findings cogently for
employers, clients, other experts, consumers, or the public.
Additionally, even trained statistical thinkers may struggle to apply
their techniques from smaller datasets to big data, as computing time
and memory limits require engineers to rethink their analytical and
visualization approaches when using databases.

This course promotes 'fluency' in 4 core data science skills relevant to
systems engineers, including (1) using relational databases, (2)
visualizing data reproducibly, (3) architecting dashboards to
communicate and summarize that data, and (4) making these reproducible
analyses with an open-source programming language.
